Copyright and takedown

Last updated: 28 October 2026

RYST Harbour hosts content that villas publish through it — their booking websites, photos, menus, messages and replies to reviews. This page says who owns what, and how to tell us if you believe something hosted through RYST Harbour infringes your rights or breaks the law. It follows the notice-and-takedown procedures of India’s Information Technology Act, 2000 (section 79 and the 2021 Rules), the Copyright Act, 1957 (section 52(1)(c) and rule 75 of the Copyright Rules, 2013) and the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act (17 U.S.C. § 512).

1. Who owns what

  • RYST Harbour’s software, design, documentation and the RYST name and logo belong to Dreamour Multiventures. RYST is a trademark of Dreamour Multiventures.
  • Everything a villa puts into RYST Harbour — its photos, text, menus, prices and guest records — stays the villa’s. The villa gives us only the licence needed to host and display it as part of the service.
  • Guest feedback and reviews remain the guest’s; the villa publishes them with the guest’s permission.

3. What a notice must contain

  1. Your name, postal address, phone number and email, and whom you represent if you act for someone else.
  2. The work you say is infringed — a description, or a link to where it is lawfully published — and proof that you own or control the rights.
  3. The exact address (URL) of the content on a site hosted through RYST Harbour that you want removed.
  4. A statement that you believe in good faith that the use is not authorised by the rights owner, its agent or the law.
  5. A statement, under penalty of perjury (or on affidavit, in India), that the information in the notice is accurate and that you are the rights owner or authorised to act for them.
  6. Your physical or electronic signature.

For other unlawful content (defamation, privacy, impersonation, content harmful to children and the other categories in Rule 3(1)(b) of the IT Rules, 2021), send the same details, a copy or link to the content and why it is unlawful; a court order or a direction from a competent authority where the law requires one.

4. What we do

  • We acknowledge a complete notice within 24 hours.
  • For a clear copyright claim we remove or disable the content within 36 hours and tell the villa that published it, giving it a chance to respond. Where a court order is required under Indian law we will say so and act on the order.
  • We resolve the complaint within 15 days and tell you the outcome.
  • We keep a record of notices, and we close the accounts of villas that repeatedly infringe others’ rights.

A knowingly false notice can make you liable for damages (17 U.S.C. § 512(f); and under Indian law).

5. Counter-notice

If your content was removed and you believe that was a mistake or misidentification, send a counter-notice to the agent above with: your contact details; the content removed and where it appeared; a statement under penalty of perjury that you believe in good faith it was removed by mistake or misidentification; your consent to the jurisdiction of the courts at Chennai, Tamil Nadu (for a DMCA counter-notice, the US federal court for your district, or Chennai if you are outside the US) and to accept service from the complainant; and your signature. We forward it to the complainant. Unless they tell us within 10 business days that they have filed a court action, we may restore the content.

6. Grievance Officer

Our Grievance Officer under the IT Rules, 2021 is Yuvanesan M, at the address above. Complaints are acknowledged within 24 hours and resolved within 15 days. See also our contact page.
